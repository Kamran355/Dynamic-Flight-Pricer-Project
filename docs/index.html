A dynamic pricing engine for general aviation

AirSide Dynamic Pricer estimates fair, cost aware pricing for private flights by combining statistical demand modeling, constrained optimization, and reinforcement learning: three methods drawn from an industrial engineering curriculum and applied to a real operational problem.

How it works

Three systems working together

A single price recommendation is the output of a short pipeline. Each stage builds on the one before it, and the system as a whole improves the more it is used.

Layer One
Learning what passengers will pay
Every time a price is quoted, whether it is accepted or declined becomes a data point. The system uses this growing history to estimate how likely a given price is to be accepted, taking into account how far in advance the flight was booked, how long the flight is, and how many people are flying. The more flights it prices, the sharper this estimate becomes.
Layer Two
Staying grounded in real costs
Every recommendation has to make sense against what it actually costs to fly the airplane: fuel, the aircraft's hourly operating cost, and how much weight it can safely carry. The system always works out the cheapest way to fuel the trip and will never recommend a price below true cost, while still searching for the price that earns the most given what it has learned about demand.
Layer Three
Thinking beyond a single flight
Rather than treating each quote in isolation, the system also reasons about the bigger picture. A reinforcement learning component lets it develop pricing habits over time that balance revenue on today's flight against the value of demand it builds for tomorrow, similar in spirit to how airlines and ride sharing platforms manage pricing at scale, applied here to general aviation.

Try your own price

The slider's range deliberately extends below the actual cost of the flight, so you can see what happens at either extreme. The recommended price is not the price most likely to be accepted, that would simply be the cheapest one. It is the price where the chance of acceptance and the margin earned if accepted together produce the highest expected profit. The tick mark shows that point, and the slider will catch there as you pass near it.
